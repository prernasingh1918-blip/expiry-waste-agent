# Kitchen Food Safety & Waste Management Policy
**Restaurant Operations Manual — Section 7: Ingredient & Prep Shelf-Life Management**
*Version 1.0*

## Purpose

This policy defines how kitchen staff and stock-management systems should
assess ingredient and prepared-item freshness, decide when items are at risk
of spoilage, and determine the correct action (use, convert, discount,
write-off). Categories differ significantly in risk profile, so rules are
category-specific rather than uniform.

---

## Quick Reference — At-Risk Thresholds by Category

| Category | At-Risk Trigger | Usage Pace Considered? | Past Use-By |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seafood | 2 days before use-by | No | Auto-write-off, no exceptions |
| Meat & Poultry | 30% shelf life left OR <75% projected use | Yes | Auto-write-off |
| Dairy (raw) | 20% shelf life left | No | Auto-write-off |
| Dairy-based preps | 24 hours from prep, hard limit | No | Auto-write-off |
| Produce | <70% projected use by use-by | Yes | Auto-write-off |
| Dry Goods & Spices | Within 7 days of use-by | No | Flag for human review, not auto-write-off |
| Bakery & Baked Goods | See Section 7.6a | Yes | Discount/staff-use first, not auto-write-off |
| Prepped items (default) | 48 hours from prep | Yes | Auto-write-off |

---

## Section 7.1 — Seafood

Seafood carries the highest food-safety risk of any category due to rapid
bacterial growth, even under proper refrigeration. Risk must be flagged
early, regardless of how quickly stock is being used.

- **At-risk threshold:** Flag any seafood batch **2 days before** its
  use-by date, regardless of current usage pace. Usage pace is not a
  reliable safety signal for seafood — a batch can look "on track" by
  volume and still be unsafe to serve close to its use-by date.
- **Convert-to-prep guidance:** Seafood nearing the at-risk window should
  be prioritized into same-day specials rather than held for later prep.
- **Write-off rule:** Any seafood batch past its use-by date must be
  written off immediately. No exceptions, no discounting for staff meals
  or donation.

## Section 7.2 — Meat & Poultry

- **At-risk threshold:** Flag when remaining shelf life is **30% or less**
  of total shelf life from receipt, OR usage pace suggests fewer than
  **75%** of units will be used by the use-by date — whichever comes first.
- **Convert-to-prep guidance:** Poultry and meat nearing risk should be
  prioritized for prep that extends shelf life (e.g., marinating, cooking
  down into a sauce base) rather than held raw.
- **Write-off rule:** Past use-by date, auto-write-off. If discoloration
  or odor is reported by staff before the use-by date, treat as an
  immediate write-off regardless of date (this policy layer assumes
  date-based tracking only; sensory checks remain a manual staff
  responsibility).

## Section 7.3 — Dairy

- **At-risk threshold:** Flag when remaining shelf life is **20% or less**
  of total shelf life from receipt.
- **Prepped dairy items — critical rule:** Any prepped item containing
  dairy (sauces, creams, dressings) **must be used within 24 hours of
  preparation**, regardless of the use-by date of the raw dairy ingredient
  it was made from. This is a hard rule — do not extend based on usage
  pace.

## Section 7.4 — Produce

- **At-risk threshold:** Flag when usage pace suggests fewer than **70%**
  of units will be used by the use-by date.
- **Note on variability:** Produce shelf life varies significantly by
  sub-type (leafy greens vs. root vegetables). Where sub-type-level shelf
  life data is not available, use the use-by date provided at receipt as
  the authoritative figure.
- **Convert-to-prep guidance:** Produce nearing risk is generally safe to
  convert into prep (chopped, sauced, roasted) to extend usable life by
  1–2 additional days, except where the resulting prep falls under
  Section 7.3 (dairy-based) or 7.5 (spice/seasoning-based shelf life
  rules).

## Section 7.5 — Dry Goods & Spices

- **At-risk threshold:** Flag only when within **7 days** of use-by date.
  Usage pace is not a meaningful signal for this category given long shelf
  life; do not flag based on usage pace alone.
- **Write-off rule:** Past use-by date, flag for review rather than
  auto-write-off — many dry goods remain safe and usable past printed
  dates, and a human should confirm before discarding.

## Section 7.6a — Bakery & Baked Goods

Bakery items fail differently from other categories: staleness, not
food safety, is usually the limiting factor. Treat with a softer action
ladder than perishables.

- **At-risk threshold:** Flag when usage pace suggests fewer than **60%**
  of units will be used by use-by date, OR when within **1 day** of
  use-by, whichever comes first.
- **Action preference:** For at-risk bakery stock, prefer **discount or
  staff-use** over write-off — baked goods remain safe to eat well past
  peak freshness, so write-off should be a last resort, not a default.
- **Write-off rule:** Do not auto-write-off past use-by date. Flag for
  human review — staff should assess texture/staleness directly before
  discarding.

## Section 7.6 — Prepped Items — General Rules

Prepped items are made from one or more raw ingredient batches and
generally carry a **shorter use-by window** than their raw components.

- Default prep shelf life (where not overridden by a category-specific
  rule above): **48 hours** from prep time.
- Dairy-containing preps: see Section 7.3 (24-hour rule overrides the
  default).
- **Traceability requirement:** Every prepped item must record which raw
  batch(es) it was made from. This enables review of whether prep timing
  was reactive (made from stock that was already at risk) versus planned.

---

## Section 7.7 — Ambiguous Cases — Escalation Rule

Do not guess when confidence is low. Flag for a clarifying question
instead of assigning an action when any of the following apply:

- The ingredient or prep type has no established usage-pace history
  (e.g., a newly introduced menu item).
- Usage pace has shifted sharply (more than 50% change) from its recent
  average without an identifiable cause (e.g., known promotion, known
  menu change).
- A raw batch has partial data missing (e.g., quantity used not logged).

---

## Section 7.8 — Cost & Waste Reporting

For every write-off or discount action, log:
- Batch/Prep ID
- Category
- Quantity and unit cost lost
- Reason (auto-write-off / staff-flagged / ambiguous-escalated-then-written-off)

This log is the basis for weekly waste-cost reporting to management.
