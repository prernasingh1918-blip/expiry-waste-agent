How it actually decides

It isn't following one fixed checklist. For every item, it works through the same questions a good kitchen manager would — in whatever order makes sense for that item.

01

How much time is left?

Checks the use-by date against today.

02

Is it being used fast enough?

Compares how much has sold or been used against how much time has passed.

03

What does the kitchen's rulebook say?

Looks up the actual safety rule for that ingredient — seafood, dairy, and spices all behave differently.

04

What should happen next?

Decides: leave it, cook it into today's menu, discount it, write it off, or ask a person.

Three real decisions it made

Pulled straight from a test run — not made up for the demo. Here's the reasoning behind three of them, in plain terms.

Seafood · 1 day left

Prawns

Batch RB-0027
Decision Use today, don't hold
Seafood spoils fast even when it looks fine on paper — so the rule is to act early, regardless of how much stock is left. Push it into today's specials.
Safety catch

Chicken Marinade

Made from Batch RB-0023
Decision Stop — needs review
The marinade itself still looked fine timing-wise. But it was made from chicken that had already expired before it was prepped — a real problem a simple timer would have completely missed.
Dairy · early warning

Fresh Cream

Batch RB-0016
Decision Keep an eye on it
Plenty of shelf life left, but usage is unusually slow — a sign the kitchen may have over-ordered. Not urgent yet, but worth watching before it becomes a problem.

Built with a real kitchen rulebook the AI reads and reasons over, not a fixed set of hardcoded rules — so it can explain, in each case, exactly why it made the call it did.
